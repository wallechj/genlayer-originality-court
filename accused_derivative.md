# The Watchmaker's Son

Tomas grew up above his father's workshop in Copperton, where the tower clock had marked every hour of his life.

When his father's eyesight failed, Tomas inherited the keys to the tower. The town depended on those bells: market days, school hours, the evening curfew all ran on them.

One winter the clock jammed at midnight. A crowd formed in the square, anxious, because a silent clock meant a town without rhythm. Tomas went up the narrow stairs with a borrowed torch and discovered that a gear had cracked under the frost.

Working through the night, he shaped a replacement from an old brass plate and set it into place just before dawn. He gave the mechanism two careful winds. At sunrise the bells sounded again, and the square broke into applause.

That spring the town held its first clock festival, and Tomas opened the tower to visitors, showing children how the gears turned.
