# The Cartographer's Map

Nia was the cartographer's apprentice, and the valley beyond the river had never been mapped.

Her master kept the old survey charts in a locked chest, and when his leg broke on the mountain path, Nia took the theodolite and set out alone. She carried dried figs, a brass compass, and her master's warning: never trust a river that runs uphill on paper.

For three weeks she walked the ridge lines, marking streams and goat trails, sleeping under pine branches. On the twenty-first day she found the pass her master had searched for all his life — a low saddle between two peaks, hidden behind a waterfall.

She returned with a full chart, inked by firelight, and the valley finally had a name on the map.
